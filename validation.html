The model takes the DTN forecast sea state and the measured vessel heading and predicts the standard deviation (σ) of heave, pitch and roll. These predictions were compared with the rig MRU (TrendData export, one sample every 5.6 min) in ±3-hour windows around each forecast step with lead time ≤ 30 h. Heave and pitch are reproduced with correlations of 0.85 and 0.81 and about three quarters of cases within ±30 %. Roll correlation is 0.56 in-sample and falls to 0.15 when the calibration month is held out, so roll is reported as indicative only. The rig cannot measure waves, so the comparison tests the forecast and the model together; the forecast wind against the rig anemometer (correlation 0.83, ratio 1.09) supports the forecast as input.

Model vs MRU, step by step

Left: measured σ (dots) and model σ from the forecast (line) over the period; the gap between 22 Sep and 6 Oct is a gap in the forecast archive, not in the MRU. Right: scatter with the 1:1 line (yellow) and ±30 % band (dotted). Pitch and roll were detrended with a 6-hour rolling mean before computing σ, to remove slow trim/heel changes; heave was not.

Prediction = √(floor² + (scale·σ_model)²). The floor is what the MRU shows when the sea is nearly flat (sensor noise plus slow heel/trim changes from DP and ballast). Scale factors (0.92 heave, 1.08 pitch, 0.90 roll) were chosen so that the largest 25 % of events are matched, which makes the model run ~10 % high in calm weather; a pure least-squares fit would give 0.79 / 0.90 / 0.53 and under-predict the big events. For an operational tool the big events matter more, so the conservative choice was kept.

Reference points: using forecast Hs alone (no motion model) gives correlations of 0.54 / 0.37 / 0.34, and the uncalibrated model gives 0.84 / 0.80 / 0.54, so the skill comes from the physics, not from the calibration.

Where the data came from

  • Forecasts: 37 DTN Marine Daily Planner issues (8 Sep – 17 Oct 2026), 976 six-hourly rows, position 22.59 S 40.23 W; only rows with lead time ≤ 30 h were used (207).
  • Motions: rig MRU heave, pitch, roll and IAS heading, 12,342 instantaneous samples at 5.6-min intervals (30 Aug – 17 Oct 2026). σ in a ±3 h window has a sampling uncertainty of about ±9 %.
  • Wind: IAS anemometer vs DTN 10-m wind: correlation 0.83, median ratio 1.09 (anemometer is higher than 10 m).
  • Vessel data: CargoMax stability reports of 15 Oct (TRT connected) and 18 Oct 2026 (disconnected), General Arrangement PBS-J-XD-00027, topside Operations Manual.
  • Independent wave data: none available for the period. Campos Basin buoys BM02 and AX24 (PNBOIA/REMObs) are offline; the nearest live buoy is the Watchkeeper at Cabo Frio, 227 km away, behind a token-gated API. A GFS-Wave or Copernicus hindcast at the rig position is the next step.

Validated envelope

Hs 1.1–2.9 m; heave up to 1.56 m peak-to-peak (95th percentile 0.95 m); rig heading 0–45° in 126 of 207 steps, so the dominant swell was mostly from astern or the quarter (head seas in only 9 steps). Head-seas results rely on the model's head/following symmetry and have not been checked against data. The colour thresholds on the calculator (2.0 m p-p, 1.0°, 1.5°) lie above everything observed so far. Helideck heave rate (derived from pitch at 113 m forward of the centre of gravity) has not been compared with HMS records yet.

Independent review

Before delivery the model, code and statistics were re-checked by a separate reviewer who had not seen them being built. Everything quantitative was recomputed from the delivered files: Python and JavaScript implementations agree to 1.4·10⁻¹⁵; JONSWAP spectra integrate to Hs within 0.3 %; heading conventions, unit conversions and the significant/maximum definitions are consistent; the statistics above reproduce to the last digit. Findings and what was done:

  • criticalBest heading picked stern-to-swell. At zero speed the model is symmetric, so a heading and its reciprocal tie; the sweep kept the first one. Fixed: ties resolve to the bow-to-weather side and the page says so.
  • criticalRoll hold-out skill not shown. Added here and on the calculator; roll is labelled indicative.
  • should fixIn-sample statistics on a half-duplicated sample; only the flattering bias shown. Both tables above now carry the overall bias, the hold-out hit rates and the 108-window count.
  • should fixValidated range narrower than the colour thresholds. Stated on both pages.
  • should fixParser dropped rows with "CALM" wind. Fixed; the page now warns when lines are missing.
  • should fixBroad swell spreading (cos²) and a roll damping ratio of 0.22 are effective, not physical, values. Kept, documented; the heading sensitivity of the index is modest (0.55–0.90 across all headings in the tests).
  • minorkyy shown but unused (removed from the display); wind-sea partitions with Tp < 4 s partly outside the frequency grid (no motion effect); roll natural period 13.7–14.8 s depending on solid vs corrected GM.

Full review text and all data files are in the model package kept by the author.

Method in brief

Heave and pitch: closed-form box-hull expressions of Jensen, Mansour & Pedersen (2004, Ocean Engineering 31, 61–85) with block/waterplane corrections and an effective sectional added-mass ratio of 0.8 (fitted). Roll: linear one-degree-of-freedom response, ω_n = √(g·GM)/k′xx with kxx = 16.2 m from the item-level weight distribution of the stability reports, effective wave-slope excitation, equivalent damping ratio 0.22 (fitted). Sea state: wind sea plus two swells as JONSWAP spectra (DNV-RP-C205 γ from Tp/√Hs) with cos² spreading; wind-sea period from Tp = 4√Hs bounded by the fully developed sea. Point motions at the MRU (30 m from CG, fitted), the main well (7 m) and the helideck (113 m) as root-sum-square of heave and x·pitch, which is exact for this model because heave and pitch are in quadrature. Statistics: σ = √m0, significant = 2σ (4σ peak-to-peak for heave), most probable maximum = σ√(2 ln N). Helideck HMS probabilities: limits from NORMAM-27/DPC Rev 2 Table 1 (Class 3 helideck, AW139 as Category B, S-92 as Category A, day and night, maxima of the last 20 minutes), heave rate as the NORMAM-27 VArf (largest vertical oscillation of the helideck centre divided by half its period); Rayleigh distribution of 20-minute maxima integrated over the model's log-normal uncertainty (sdlog 0.20 pitch, 0.34 roll, 0.25 heave rate). The helideck heave rate has not been compared with HMS records yet.
